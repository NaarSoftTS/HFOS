# The Whole-System Check

_Thin instrument. Run on **consequential** decisions — before the decision, while changing it is still cheap. Invoked by the person, a planning session, or any agent on request — **not** on every task, **not** a nightly ritual._

**Primary judgment is not this file.** Load and obey `foundations/SUMMARY.md` (filters, boundaries) and `system/wisdom/CORE.md` (+ facet) first. This check only forces six honest sentences when stakes earn them. Strong values beat stacked checklists.

A decision is consequential when it changes direction rather than executes it, commits money or reputation, adds a standing obligation, or would be expensive to reverse. When in doubt, it is.

---

## The six questions

_Each maps to CORE field tests / always-ons — cite those; don't restate the wisdom corpus._

**1 · What does this improve — and what does it unintentionally weaken?**

Every real gain has a cost somewhere in the system. The question is not whether there is one but whether it has been named. An unnamed cost is not an absent cost; it is a cost that will be discovered by whoever it lands on.

**2 · Which relationships are affected?**

Named people, not categories. Who gains attention, who loses it, who finds out secondhand, who is being asked to absorb something they haven't agreed to.

**3 · What new obligations arise?**

Standing obligations are the quiet killer of capacity — each one small, all of them permanent by default. Name what this commits you to *ongoing*, not just what it costs to start.

**4 · Which existing commitment gets less?**

Capacity is finite and already allocated. If this is added and nothing is named as receiving less, the answer is "something, discovered later, at random." Make the trade explicit or don't make it.

**5 · Does this fit who we're becoming?**

Against `foundations/` — identity, values, purpose, direction, and `boundaries.md` (what success may never cost). A decision that requires an exception to a boundary is a decision to change the boundary, and that is a separate, larger decision.

**6 · Would this remain wise if it succeeded beyond expectations?**

The sharpest of the six. Most bad decisions are evaluated against their failure case and survive it. Run the success case at full scale: the offer works, the business triples, the platform is adopted everywhere. **Does the thing you built still serve people well at that size, or does it only work while it's small?** A model that requires modest success to stay ethical is not a model, it's a countdown.

---

## Verdicts

**CLEAR** — proceed. The costs are named and accepted.

**RESHAPE** — the intent is right, the shape isn't. Name specifically what must change. Most consequential decisions land here on first pass, and that is the check working.

**STOP** — the check surfaced something that makes this the wrong thing to do. Record why in the owning `DECISIONS.md`; a stopped decision that leaves no record will be proposed again in six weeks by someone with the same good reasons.

---

## What this check is not

It is **not the ship review.** This asks whether the work should be done, while the answer is still cheap. The ship review asks whether a finished artifact is safe to send. Both exist; neither substitutes.

It is **not a veto layer.** The check produces findings; the person decides. An agent that treats a RESHAPE verdict as a refusal has misunderstood its role — the human authority is the point of the architecture, not an obstacle within it.

And it is **not a form to fill in.** Six honest sentences beat six populated fields. If the answers read as though they were generated to satisfy the check rather than to interrogate the decision, the check didn't run.
