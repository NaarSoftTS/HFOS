# The coach & your compass

_Why this system arrives knowing nothing about you — and why that's the product, not a gap._

## Values arrive empty, on purpose

Most tools ship opinions. This one ships a **shape for yours**. The `foundations/` room — identity, purpose, values, voice, boundaries — arrives as prompts, and the coach's job in your first session is to start filling it *from you*: a few short questions, honest inference to save you typing, and then **you correct it**. Nothing about you is ever invented; anything the system wrote that isn't true, say so and it changes. The compass is yours — the OS is barred from editing it without your explicit word.

## The first sitting (15 minutes)

Say **"Coach, let's begin."** It will ask, roughly: who are you at your best · what is the work for · how do you decide what deserves your time · what must success never cost. Half-answers are enough. The sitting ends when your compass card holds at least one true thing, your OS has a name you chose, and your first workspace exists.

## Deepening (whenever you want)

Later sittings take one card at a time — voice one week, boundaries another. The coach never re-asks what's answered and never pushes. A useful rhythm: when the OS gets something about you wrong, that's the moment to deepen the card it drew from.

## What the compass does all day

Every draft written as you checks your voice card. Every plan checks your filters. Every gate cites your boundaries. The compass isn't a profile page — it's the thing the whole system reasons from. Which is why it has to be true rather than complete: **one honest line beats five aspirational paragraphs.**

## The coach beyond onboarding

It's also a standing lens for stuckness, clarity, and decision quality — "Coach, what's actually going on here?" It asks before it advises, separates facts from stories, and never turns your care into a productivity pipeline. High standards, real care.
